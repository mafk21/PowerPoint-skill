# 12 — Storytelling & Persuasion Frameworks

Design gets attention; structure gets decisions. These are the frameworks the
profession's authorities (Duarte, Minto/McKinsey, Knaflic, Garr Reynolds) converge
on — compiled to plug into `04-deck-structures.md`. Pick ONE primary framework
per deck (they composite well as layers, noted at the end).

## 1 · The Big Idea (Nancy Duarte, *Resonate*) — start here, always

Before any outline, write one sentence that:
1. states your **unique point of view**,
2. conveys **what's at stake**,
3. is a **complete sentence**.

> Weak: "Q3 sales update." Strong: "If we don't fix onboarding this quarter,
> growth stalls below the line we promised the board."

Audience is the **hero**, you are the mentor (Yoda, not Luke). Interview the
room first: their frustrations, goals, vocabulary — the deck must describe
*their* world before asking them to change it.

## 2 · Duarte's Presentation Sparkline — "What is ↔ What could be"

Every great talk analyzed (MLK, Jobs' iPhone launch…) oscillates:

```
WHAT IS → ⚡gap → WHAT COULD BE → what is → what could be → … → NEW BLISS → CTA
(open ≤10%)   (call to adventure)   (middle: keep toggling)     (call to action)
```

Rules: make the **gap as wide as honestly possible** (a narrow gap = a status
report); change mode every few minutes (never > ~5 min in one register);
contrast in 3 layers — **content** (is/could-be), **emotion** (analytical ↔
human story), **delivery** (slides ↔ demo/pause/prop). End on a vivid "new
bliss" picture + explicit choice. Statistical garnish: in a classic study of
476 political speeches, ~half of all applause followed moments of contrast.
**Deck translation:** alternate problem slides and solution/future slides
throughout instead of all-problems-then-all-solutions; every section returns
to the tension once more before resolving.

**S.T.A.R. moment** — Something They'll Always Remember: engineer ONE peak
(shocking stat → our R9 hero stat; evocative visual; repeatable soundbite;
emotive story; live dramatization). Place it where attention sags (~60–75%
through) — pair with your biggest Morph moment. Peak–end rule (`10`) says
this + your closer are what they'll retell.

## 3 · Minto Pyramid + SCQA (McKinsey method) — for decision decks

When the room must *decide* (execs, boards, steering committees), invert the
journey: **answer first**.

- **Pyramid**: governing thought (recommendation) on top → 3–4 supporting
  arguments (the magic of 3–4 = working-memory limit) → evidence beneath.
- **MECE test**: arguments Mutually Exclusive (no overlap) + Collectively
  Exhaustive (no gaps). Failing MECE = the insight is fuzzy.
- **SCQA opener** (4 sentences max): **S**ituation (agreed context) →
  **C**omplication (what changed/broke) → **Q**uestion (implied) → **A**nswer
  (= pyramid top). Makes answer-first feel natural, not abrupt.
- **Action titles**: every slide title is a complete-sentence conclusion
  (≤ ~15 words, active voice, quantified) — titles read top-to-bottom must
  *be* the argument (this is Knaflic's horizontal logic, below).
- **Ghost deck**: build the full storyline as titles-only first; get structure
  approved before designing a single slide.
- Deck shape: cover → exec summary (pyramid in miniature) → body (one MECE
  section per argument, each opening with its conclusion) → recommendations
  (numbered, verb-led, owner + date) → appendix (referenced, not presented).
- A senior reader must "get the ask" from slide 1–2 alone.

## 4 · Knaflic's data-story method (*Storytelling with Data*)

Six lessons: ① understand context (audience, ask, risk) ② choose the right
visual ③ eliminate clutter ④ focus attention (pre-attentive attributes:
color/size/position — our strategic gray) ⑤ think like a designer
(affordance, accessibility, aesthetics, acceptance) ⑥ tell a story
(beginning → middle tension → end with call to action).

Clarity tools to run before building:
- **3-minute story**: if you had 3 minutes with no slides, what would you say?
  (If you can't, you don't know your message yet.)
- **Big Idea** (Duarte's, above) in one sentence.
- **Storyboard** on paper/thumbnails before opening PowerPoint.

QA tools after building (pairwise with `06`):
- **Horizontal logic** — titles alone tell the story (action titles required).
- **Vertical logic** — everything on a slide reinforces its title; title
  promises, slide proves.
- **Reverse storyboard** — flip the finished deck, write each slide's point;
  the list must match your intended outline.
- **Fresh perspective** — a naive reader narrates back what mattered; fix gaps.
- Always **make a recommendation** — even a wrong one beats "here's the data,"
  because it forces the productive conversation.

## 5 · Presentation Zen (Garr Reynolds) — for talks/keynotes

Restraint, simplicity, naturalness (Zen aesthetics): signal-vs-noise ratio as
a moral duty; empty space is a design element; one powerful image > seven
bullets; slides are the *backdrop*, the speaker is the presentation. Prepare
away from the computer (analog storyboarding); design for the back row;
deliver with presence. Use when the deck is *performed* live (conference,
lecture, TED-style) rather than read or decided over.

## 6 · Choose your layer-cake

| Situation | Primary | Add |
|---|---|---|
| Investor pitch | Duarte sparkline (problem↔future oscillation) | Big Idea opener; STAR = traction reveal; `04-A` order |
| Board/steering decision | Minto pyramid + SCQA | Knaflic QA (horizontal logic); appendix discipline |
| Data review / QBR | Knaflic 6 lessons | Minto exec-summary-first; hero-stat STAR beats |
| Thesis defense | Assertion–evidence (`10`§3) | Zen restraint; sparing STAR (key finding) |
| Keynote / seminar | Reynolds Zen | Duarte contrast rhythm; narrative STAR |
| Sales proposal | SCQA (client's world first) | Sparkline toggling pain↔outcome per module |

Universal constants across all frameworks (already laws in this skill):
answer the audience's "so what" every slide; titles carry conclusions;
emotion and analysis alternate; end with a specific ask. And Duarte's ethical
line, worth keeping: these tools are powerful — her cautionary case is Enron
using beautiful presentations as propaganda. Persuade honestly.
