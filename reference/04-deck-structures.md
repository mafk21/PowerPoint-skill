# 04 — Deck Structures (story arcs per use case)

PlacePlate sells by *use case* (Business / Academic / Pro pitch). These are the
narrative structures their guides and products are built around. Pick the arc
first, then fill it with recipes from `03-slide-recipes.md`. Story rule that
applies to every arc: **headline-only test** — reading just the slide headlines
in order must tell the whole story.

Global pacing laws (from their guides):

- **~1 slide per minute** of speaking (academic); pitches run faster.
- 20 min talk → **10–15 slides** (≈90 s each). 30+ slides = failed prioritization.
- Cut test: "If I could keep only 10 slides, which ones?" The rest → appendix.
- Front-load proof: investors decide in ~3 minutes; panels judge early polish.
- Morph "big moments" (3–5) go at: opener, 1–2 section changes, data climax, ask.

---

## A · Investor pitch deck (12 slides — their flagship PP-1 structure)

Purpose: **earn the next meeting**, not close the round in the room.
(Kawasaki 10/20/30 as spirit: few slides, short talk, big type.)

| # | Slide (recipe) | Job / content rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title (R1) | Company, one-line tagline (what + for whom), presenter, date |
| 2 | Problem (R4) | Specific, painful; data point or micro-story. Make them *feel* it |
| 3 | Solution (R4/R5) | 1–2 sentences + product visual. If it needs a paragraph, simplify |
| 4 | Market (R10) | TAM/SAM/SOM, bottom-up, cited. $1B+ TAM gets attention |
| 5 | Product (R4 image) | Screenshots/demo. Show, don't tell — real UI beats mockups |
| 6 | Business model (R5/R7) | Exact price & mechanism ("$X/user/month"), unit economics |
| 7 | Traction (R8/R9) | Revenue/users/growth/LOIs. **Strong traction? Move to #3–4** |
| 8 | Go-to-market (R6) | Channels + acquisition cost + why it scales |
| 9 | Competition (R7 or 2×2) | Never "no competition". Acknowledge, then differentiate |
| 10 | Team (R11) | Unfair advantage: domain expertise, prior exits, advisors |
| 11 | Financials (R8) | 3–5 yr visual projections + stated assumptions. Charts, not sheets |
| 12 | The Ask (R13) | Amount + use of funds + milestone it unlocks. Specific & confident |

Variants: their startup-pitch guide compresses this to **10** (merge GTM into
traction/model; financials fold into the ask). Seed decks: traction early.
Series A+: add unit-economics and use-of-funds detail slides before the ask.

Pitch-specific death list: burying traction at the end; vague "$100B market";
"we have no competition"; hockey-stick numbers with no assumptions; forgetting
to actually ask; >15 slides.

## B · Academic / thesis defense (10–12 slides per 10 min)

Mirrors the research paper, compressed. Credibility > decoration. Best served
by restrained palettes (royal-blue / aqua-neumorphic themes).

| # | Slide (recipe) | Job / content rules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title (R1) | Title, name, institution, course/program, date. Institution colors = bonus formality |
| 2 | Outline (R2) | 4–6 sections. Roadmap keeps the panel oriented |
| 3 | Introduction & background (R4) | Why this research matters; the gap it fills |
| 4 | Research questions / objectives (R4/R5) | Explicit RQs — panels grade against these later |
| 5 | Methodology (R6) | Flowchart of how the work was done; visuals > bullets |
| 6–8 | Results & discussion (R8/R9) | One finding per slide; key result highlighted; axes labeled with units |
| 9 | Conclusion (R4) | 3–5 takeaways answering the RQs; end on the "so what" |
| 10 | References (bonus recipe) | Institution's citation style |
| + | Backup slides | 2–3 deeper-data slides after references for Q&A |

Delivery system (from their academic guide): practice aloud ≥3× (not in your
head); present with Presenter View; pause ~5 s after revealing a chart before
explaining; park off-scope questions on a "parking lot" slide and return at end.
Formatting floors are stricter here (24 pt body / 36 pt headings / 18 pt floor).

## C · Business presentation / company profile (12–15 slides)

Their Best-for-Business line + custom-service work ("Company Profile" previews).

1. Title (R1) — company + value proposition + occasion
2. Agenda (R2)
3. Who we are (R4) — one-line positioning + founding/scale proof (R9 stat strip)
4. The problem in our market (R4/R8) — why now
5. What we do / services (R5)
6. How it works / process (R6)
7. Proof: results & metrics (R8 + R9)
8. Case study / client story (R4 image + R12 quote)
9. Differentiation (R7 vs typical alternatives)
10. Clients & partners (logo wall via R5)
11. Team (R11)
12. Roadmap / plans (R6 timeline variant)
13. Offer/packages or next steps (R7 pricing variant if selling)
14. Closing + contact (R13)

## D · Sales / proposal deck (10–12 slides)

Client-centric re-ordering of C: their pain first, you second.

1. Title with **client's** name/logo prominent (R1)
2. Their situation & goals (R4) — prove you listened
3. Cost of inaction (R9/R8)
4. Proposed solution (R5)
5. Implementation plan (R6 with dates)
6. Expected outcomes (R8, conservative numbers)
7. Proof: similar-client results (R12 + R9)
8. Why us (R7)
9. Investment & options (R7 pricing, hero column = recommended)
10. Risks & mitigation (R4) — builds trust, steals objections
11. Next steps with dates (R13)

## E · Seminar / training / conference talk

1. Title (R1) → 2. Hook stat or story (R9/R12) → 3. Agenda (R2) →
per section: divider (R3) + concept (R4) + example/data (R8) + mini-recap →
synthesis (R5 "if you remember 3 things") → closing + resources (R13).
For teaching: reveal content progressively (Appear animations or Morph
duplicates), one concept per slide, generous examples.

## F · Monthly/quarterly business review (data-forward)

1. Title + period (R1) → 2. Executive summary = 3 hero stats (R9) →
3–8. One KPI story per slide (R8, assertion titles: "Churn fell below 2%") →
9. Misses & causes (R4, honest) → 10. Initiatives (R6) → 11. Decisions needed
(R13 as ask). Appendix: full tables.

## Choosing & sizing the deck (their selection framework)

1. **Tone → audience first** (academic ≠ pitch ≠ client ≠ creative).
2. **Slide count ≈ minutes available**; pick a structure above and don't exceed
   it by >20% — move overflow to appendix/backup slides.
3. **Layout variety check**: the deck needs at least one each of divider, data,
   grid/process and closing recipes — 20 lookalike content slides read as a doc.
4. **Editability check** (when using templates): live text boxes, editable
   shapes, image placeholders, Master Slide present.
5. **License check**: personal vs commercial vs no-resale — know which you hold.
