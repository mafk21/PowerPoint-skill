## Patterns across ALL of them (= the meta-playbook)

1. **Morph is the genre.** Every top account's biggest posts are Morph
   transformations. Motion *is* the content; design is the excuse.
2. **Hooks are formulas**: "Did you know this was possible in PowerPoint?",
   "POV: you created a cute PowerPoint using Morph", "It's [year]… time to
   learn the morph transition", "Comment PART 2".
3. **Funnel**: 15–30 s reel → link-in-bio → free sampler → $3–13 template →
   bundle/course → (top) done-for-you service. PlacePlate runs it exactly.
4. **Teach one move per video**, full build in ≤60 s, captions carry the steps
   (sound-off friendly), original audio for re-use.
5. **Themed skins travel**: Netflix UI, Instagram UI, anime — recognizable
   worlds reskinned as decks outperform generic corporate slides.
6. **Free + pro pairing**: every seller gives a 5-slide freebie to prove the
   Morph works on *your* machine (kills the #1 purchase fear: compatibility).
7. **Hashtag spine**: `#powerpoint #morphtransition #powerpointpresentation
   #powerpointtemplate #presentation #tutorial #studytok`.

## How this skill uses the atlas

- **Technique backlog** → `11-advanced-powerpoint-techniques.md` distills their
  moves (3D morph, parallax, Zoom hubs, logo reveals, rotation motifs…).
- **Content requests** ("make me a reel/carousel about my deck") → formulas in
  `15-prompt-playbook.md` + specs in `13-resources-tools-and-assets.md`.
- **"Like X's style" requests** → map here first: e.g. "like Jacob" =
  student-clean aesthetic Morph; "like One Skill" = interactive Zoom hub;
  "like Luis Urrutia" = playful themed skin; "like Slidor/Duarte" = agency polish.
- Respect the line: learn techniques, link originals, never re-upload their files.
